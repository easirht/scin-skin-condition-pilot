Skin-condition classification on SCIN: what a careful small study can and cannot show

Not a medical device. Nothing here predicts anything about a person's skin. There is no model to try on this page, and no SCIN image or model weight is published.

In one paragraph

I trained and evaluated image models on 1,172 dermatologist-labelled cases from the SCIN dataset (8 conditions, all body parts), with group-aware splits, metadata-only controls, calibration, ranking metrics and a skin-tone composition check. The honest result is modest: the models rank cases above chance, but whether they beat what contributor-reported metadata already carries depends on the metric (not at the argmax, modestly by ranking), fine-tuning is not shown to beat frozen features, and most predictions are still wrong. The first plan (facial skin conditions) did not survive contact with the data.

The data

Labels are dermatologist estimates from photographs, not biopsy results. In 30.2% of labelled cases dermatologists tied for the top condition; these were excluded, so the classes describe a consensus subset. Cases that share a byte-identical image were merged into one group for splitting.

Results: balanced accuracy

Pooled out-of-fold, 95% interval from a bootstrap over duplicate-linked groups. Chance for 8 classes is 0.125 (dashed line).

locked protocolclass-weighted control (added after seeing results)

The fine-tuned model used a class-weighted loss; the other locked-protocol models did not, so compare it with the orange rows. Class weighting raised balanced accuracy by about 0.04 to 0.07 for every baseline.

  • Image models are above chance and above body-part-only metadata.
  • At the argmax they are not distinguishable from the richer contributor-reported metadata (upper bound 0.285 when class-weighted).
  • Fine-tuning is not shown to beat class-weighted frozen features (paired differences +0.019 to +0.028, all intervals include 0).
  • Overall accuracy of the fine-tuned model is 0.294, below simply predicting Eczema (0.314).

Unweighted frozen models are overconfident (mean confidence 0.44 to 0.64 against accuracy 0.30 to 0.32). Keeping only the 20% most confident cases lifts the fine-tuned model's accuracy from 0.294 to 0.372, which still means most of those predictions are wrong. Confidence-based abstention helps a little and does not make these models reliable.

Limitations

  • Small sample, wide intervals, single annotator, labels estimated from photographs, no healthy class.
  • The v2 design followed a null v1 result. The protocol log was created after some runs and says so at its top; file times indicate order but do not prove it.
  • Several analyses (class-weight control, subgroups, calibration, AUC) were added after the main results.
  • Eczema and Allergic Contact Dermatitis overlap clinically and were kept separate. No clinical interpretation is attached to any error pattern.
